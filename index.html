Essential Oil Safety Reference

Aromatherapy is powerful medicine. Learn the rules for safe, natural wellness.

Pets Safety Warning

Many common essential oils are highly toxic to dogs and cats, who lack liver enzymes to metabolize them.

  • Toxic to cats: Eucalyptus, Tea Tree, Citrus Oils
  • Toxic to dogs: Tea Tree, Pine, Cinnamon
  • Keep diffusers in open rooms where pets can exit freely.

Pregnancy & Lactation

Some essential oils contain natural compounds that can stimulate contractions or alter hormonal balances.

  • Avoid completely: Sage, Clary Sage, Rosemary, Wintergreen
  • Generally safe after 1st Trimester: Lavender, Sweet Orange (Always at low dilution)

Phototoxicity

Cold-pressed citrus oils contain furanocoumarins, compounds that react under solar UV radiation to cause severe burns and blisters.

  • Do not apply: Lemon, Bergamot, Grapefruit before UV exposure
  • Wait at least 12 to 18 hours before sun or tanning bed exposure.

Child Safety (Under 12)

Children's skin and lungs are thin and highly sensitive. Standard adult guidelines do not apply.

  • Avoid Peppermint and Eucalyptus near the faces of babies (causes breathing inhibition).
  • Dilution limit: Max 0.25% to 0.5% dilution for children.

Core Safety Commandments

01

Do Not Ingest

Essential oils are highly concentrated. Internal use can severely damage mucus membranes and gastrointestinal systems.

02

Perform Patch Tests

Apply a single diluted drop to your forearm and cover. Check the spot after 24 hours for any redness, itchiness, or allergic response.

03

Flush with Carrier Oil

If an essential oil gets in your eye or causes skin burning, flush immediately with a carrier/cooking oil. Water will not wash it off.
